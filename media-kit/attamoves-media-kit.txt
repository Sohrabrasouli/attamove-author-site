FULL
Atta Arghandiwal is an Afghan American author, speaker, humanitarian,
and mentor whose work is rooted in a life rebuilt across countries,
languages, and institutions.

Born in Kabul, Atta served in the Afghan Air Force and worked in public
relations and sales at the Hotel Inter-Continental in Kabul. He left
Afghanistan in August 1980 after the political rupture that followed the
1978 coup and the Soviet invasion. After living as a refugee in Germany,
he arrived in the United States in December 1981 with basic English and
$10.

Atta began his American working life as a bank teller. Over the next 29
years, he built a career in banking and rose to Senior Vice President
and Regional Manager. That experience—beginning again, learning
unfamiliar systems, earning trust, supporting a family, and accepting
responsibility—became part of the foundation for his writing and
community work.

His books include Lost Decency: The Gilded Cages and the Search for Home
and The Self-Sufficient Immigrant in the Age of AI: A Field Manual for
the Work of Standing Again, co-authored with Sohrab Rasouli. Across
memoir, public conversation, and practical guidance, Atta examines
Afghanistan, exile, moral memory, work, family stability,
self-sufficiency, and the dignity of rebuilding a life.

Today, Atta writes and speaks for readers, universities, libraries,
cultural institutions, immigrant-serving organizations, and communities
seeking a serious conversation about memory, responsibility, and human
judgment in a changing world.

CURRENT BOOKS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE SELF-SUFFICIENT IMMIGRANT IN THE AGE OF AI
A Field Manual for the Work of Standing Again
Atta Arghandiwal and Sohrab Rasouli

This practical field manual is written for immigrants, refugees,
restarting families, and the people who serve beside them. It connects
the work of building stability with the realities of an AI-shaped
economy. AI is presented as a tool for preparation, comparison,
drafting, translation, learning, and organization—not as an authority.
The reader remains responsible for privacy, verification, consequential
decisions, and the human meaning of the work.

Buy: https://bit.ly/4pr3TOU

LOST DECENCY: THE GILDED CAGES AND THE SEARCH FOR HOME
Atta Arghandiwal

A work of memory, exile, and moral history, Lost Decency connects
Afghanistan's political rupture with its human cost. Through family
memory, displacement, and the search for home, Atta asks what people
carry when a country changes around them—and what dignity and
responsibility can preserve after familiar structures are gone.

INTERVIEW AND SPEAKING TOPICS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

These are the same four subjects he speaks on publicly, in the wording
approved for use.

01. Immigrant Self-Sufficiency in the Age of AI
AI can help newcomers translate, compare options, prepare questions,
organize information, and draft a first version—but it can also create
false confidence, expose private information, and produce convincing
errors. Drawing from his lived experience and the new book co-authored
with Sohrab Rasouli, Atta explores how immigrants, families, and the
people who support them can use new tools without surrendering privacy,
responsibility, or human judgment.

02. Afghanistan, Memory, and Moral Loss
Atta offers a lived account of Afghanistan before and after political
rupture, and of the human consequences that continue long after public
attention moves on. He connects national events with family memory,
displacement, exile, and the difficult responsibility to tell the truth
without turning suffering into spectacle. The conversation invites
audiences to consider what history costs ordinary people and what memory
asks of those who survive it.

03. Dignity, Work, and Rebuilding a Life
Atta arrived in the United States in December 1981 with basic English
and $10, began as a bank teller, and built a 29-year banking career that
led to Senior Vice President and Regional Manager. This talk is not a
promise that every path looks the same. It is an honest account of
learning unfamiliar systems, earning trust, accepting responsibility,
supporting a family, and preserving dignity while beginning again.

04. From Survival to Responsibility
Survival is urgent, but it cannot be the final plan. Atta examines the
shift from immediate crisis toward responsibility, structure, family
stability, and long-term participation in a community. He speaks
honestly about receiving help, learning systems, making difficult
choices, and refusing to disappear inside dependence or shame. The
conversation centers dignity: not isolation, not perfection, but the
ability to take the next responsible step.
