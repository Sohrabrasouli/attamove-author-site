Bio

From survival to responsibility.

My life trajectory has been marked by resilience, self-sufficiency, and the power of taking ownership.

As a first-generation Afghan American, I have traveled the long road from Kabul, service, displacement, and refugee life to a 29-year career as a banking executive in the United States. Today, my mission is to use that experience to challenge humanity toward a deeper sense of responsibility and service.

My formative years in Afghanistan were defined by a deep commitment to my homeland, including service in the Air Force. The political devastation of the late 1970s and the subsequent Soviet invasion forced me onto a life-altering path.

When I immigrated to the United States in December 1981, I started as a bank teller and climbed the ranks to become a Senior Vice President and Regional Manager. That professional success was never the end goal. It became the foundation for my calling: uplifting the global immigrant community and advocating for those who are often overlooked.

Chapter One: Restart

Begin with the facts of your life, not the shame around them. What papers matter this week? What appointment cannot be missed? What one action would make tomorrow less fragile?
